{# Portfolio Review: which products earn their place, and which to churn. **This replaced a CSV upload.** The previous tab asked the owner to download a Business Report from Seller Central and upload it, then wrote the aggregate to a JSON file at repo root. So the analysis was stale the moment it was saved and could not be repeated without a human. Every figure here comes from Amazon's own Economics data through Data Kiosk instead, joined to our review scraper. Six things are deliberate: 1. **Parent rows expand to pack sizes.** Amazon pools reviews across a variation family — measured, Roasted Chana 1 kg / 1.5 kg / 2 kg all report 4.2 stars from 477 reviews — so a rating cannot discriminate between sizes and sits on the parent only. Economics genuinely does differ per size, and that is where a kill decision is taken. 2. **A verdict always shows its reason.** "KILL — net -56.8% with 79% TACOS" can be checked against Seller Central and argued with; a score of 23 cannot. 3. **Every margin is PRE-COGS**, and the banner says so. Amazon's netProceeds excludes what it costs to MAKE the product, so a size at +8.8% may still lose money. 4. **Nothing here writes to Amazon.** The decision control records the owner's own judgement; turning ads off stays a human action in Seller Central. 5. **Decisions save on the spot**, like the Orders tab's packed tick: a select and a note have no half-typed state worth protecting, and a forgotten Save would lose the reasoning. 6. **A dash, never a zero.** A product with no sales has no TACOS, and "0%" would rank it among the most ad-efficient products in the portfolio. #}
